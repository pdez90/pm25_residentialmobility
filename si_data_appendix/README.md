# SI data appendix: descriptive figures removed from the Supplementary Information in revision

These figures (Figures S22–S60 of the submitted SI) map every input the analysis uses at the start and end of the study period, and show migration across each pollutant surface. They were removed from the SI at a reviewer's request and are provided here with their original captions. They are regenerated by `scripts/descriptive_maps_si.R` (maps; needs the IPUMS extract for the migration-PUMA panels) and `scripts/pollution_sankey.R` (flow diagrams); `scripts/build_si_data_appendix.R` copies them into this folder.

The within-metro flows can also be explored interactively at https://01a12bbc-84a4-a651-2a33-53f591fca244.share.connect.posit.cloud/ (tab 5 and 'Trace a neighbourhood').

## Captions

- **Figure S22:** Total PM2.5 (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group annual-mean PM2.5 from the V6.NA.01 satellite-derived product (van Donkelaar et al., 2021). Both panels share one color scale, truncated at the 2nd and 98th percentiles, so the national decline over the study period is directly visible.

- **Figure S23:** NO2 (ppb), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group annual-mean NO2 from the land-use-regression surface of (Larkin et al., 2017), scaled year by year to OMI satellite columns (Anenberg et al., 2022). NO2 is the sharpest traffic marker in the paper; its concentration in metropolitan cores is visible at this scale. Shared color scale across panels, truncated at the 2nd and 98th percentiles.

- **Figure S24:** Warm-season ozone (ppb), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group warm-season daily maximum 8-hour ozone (5). Unlike the primary combustion pollutants, ozone is formed by photochemistry downwind of sources and peaks outside urban cores, the gradient that produces the reversed avoidance gap in the main text. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S25:** Wildfire smoke PM2.5 (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group annual-mean smoke PM2.5 from (6), estimated on a 10 km grid from satellite-detected smoke plumes; block groups inherit the value of their grid cell. Smoke was episodic and concentrated in the West during the study period, with annual means near zero across most of the East, which is why it serves as the paper's negative-control exposure. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S26:** Sulfate PM2.5 component (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group sulfate component from V6.NA.01 (1). Sulfate forms from power-plant SO2 emissions; its steep decline over the period reflects coal-plant controls and retirements. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S27:** Nitrate PM2.5 component (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group nitrate component from V6.NA.01 (1). Nitrate is a secondary particle formed from the nitrogen oxides emitted by traffic and power plants. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S28:** Black carbon PM2.5 component (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group black-carbon component from V6.NA.01 (1). Black carbon marks primary combustion, dominated in cities by traffic and diesel, and is one of the pollutants with the largest avoidance and sorting estimates. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S29:** Organic matter PM2.5 component (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group organic-matter component from V6.NA.01 (1). Urban organic matter derives largely from vehicle exhaust, residential wood burning, and cooking, plus secondary formation. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S30:** Median household income (dollars), 2013 versus 2019. Block-group ACS five-year estimates; origin median income is a control in Eq. 2 and defines affordability in the opportunity-set analysis. Log-scaled color scale shared across panels, truncated at the 2nd and 98th percentiles.

- **Figure S31:** Median gross rent (dollars per month), 2013 versus 2019. Block-group ACS five-year estimates; destination rents enter the Gelbach affordability decomposition and the rent-based opportunity-set sensitivity. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S32:** Median home value (dollars), 2013 versus 2019. Block-group ACS five-year estimates; destination home values enter the Gelbach affordability decomposition, and the home-value-to-income ratio defines the affordable-clean opportunity set. Log-scaled shared color scale, truncated at the 2nd and 98th percentiles.

- **Figure S33:** Share of adults 25 and older with a bachelor's degree or higher (%), 2013 versus 2019. Block-group ACS five-year estimates; the education control in Eq. 2, and an input to the gentrification classification. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S34:** Share of the population below the federal poverty line (%), 2013 versus 2019. Block-group ACS five-year estimates; the poverty control in Eq. 2. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S35:** Black population share (%), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group ACS shares. These shares define the origin racial/ethnic contrasts used throughout the paper: an origin is majority Black when its share exceeds 50%. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S36:** Hispanic population share (%), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group ACS shares; an origin is majority Hispanic when its share exceeds 50%. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S37:** White population share (%), 2011 versus 2019. Block-group ACS shares; majority-white origins (share above 50%) are the reference group in the avoidance models. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S38:** Neighborhood racial/ethnic majority, 2011 versus 2019. Each block group is colored by its racial/ethnic majority (majority Black, majority Hispanic, majority white, or no majority), using the same 50% thresholds as the analysis. This is the paper's origin classification, mapped at its unit of analysis.

- **Figure S39:** Gentrification eligibility and gentrifying neighborhoods, 2011-2019. Top left: share of each county's block groups ever eligible for gentrification during the study period (low income and low home value relative to their county). Remaining panels: among eligible block groups, the share flagged as gentrifying at any point during 2011-2019 under each of the four measures used in the classification (neighborhood transition, market expectations, classic Freeman/Bates (14, 15), and capital reinvestment). Grey counties have no eligible block groups. Definitions appear in Supplementary Methods, Gentrification classification.

- **Figure S40:** Metropolitan Black-white residential segregation, 2011 versus 2019. The Duncan and Duncan (1955) dissimilarity index (10) computed from block-group Black and white population counts within each core-based statistical area: the share of either group that would have to change block groups to equalize the two distributions (0 = full integration, 1 = complete segregation). Non-metropolitan areas are grey. This is the index behind the segregation quartiles and interactions in Figures S16 and S17 and Tables S17 and S20.

- **Figure S41:** Migration PUMAs are Census-defined, county-based units of at least 100,000 residents, the finest origin-destination geography available in public ACS microdata, and the resolution of the individual-level replication (Tables S22-S24). This map shows the survey-weighted number of ACS respondents who moved into each migration PUMA in the year before the interview, 2012 versus 2019 (log color scale, truncated at the 2nd and 98th percentiles).

- **Figure S42:** Black (non-Hispanic) share of in-movers by migration PUMA (%), 2012 versus 2019, from ACS microdata (survey-weighted). Together with Figure S43, this shows the geography of the minority mover populations underlying the individual-level replication. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S43:** Hispanic share of in-movers by migration PUMA (%), 2012 versus 2019, from ACS microdata (survey-weighted). Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S44:** Mean PM2.5 by migration PUMA (µg/m3), 2011 versus 2019: block-group PM2.5 (1) aggregated to migration PUMAs with block-group population weights. This is the exposure surface at the resolution used in the individual-level replication; comparing it with Figure S22 shows how much fine-scale variation the coarser geography averages away. Shared, percentile-truncated color scale.

- **Figure S45:** Migration across the total PM2.5 surface: origin quintile to destination quintile. Alluvial diagram of MIGRATE block-group flows pooled over 2011-2019. Each move's origin and destination block groups are assigned to national rank-based quintiles of total PM2.5 (µg/m3) within the move year (Q1 cleanest to Q5 dirtiest), and ribbon width is proportional to the number of movers (flow-weighted). The diagram shows descriptively how migration redistributes movers across the total PM2.5 distribution.
